## 2016

+ When I first entered the school in my freshman year, I was curious about everything. At that time, my advisor, who was already a junior at that time, learned about the school's Android lab with us. As the vice chairman of the Youth League Committee of the college, he Possess exceptional personal abilities. Under his propaganda, I also entered the Android Lab with a curious attitude to study.

+ When I entered the Android Lab in 2016, through a week of training, I briefly learned the C language, but I still need to consolidate the school curriculum. Through Mr. Jing Chao's explanation in the class, I really understood and understood the path of programming.

+ In 2016, I not only learned C language, but also gradually came into contact with Java programming and development, because Android development is actually written in Java language (in the beginning, now there is kotlin). So I continue to learn Java development while following the weekly training in Android Labs. In the last winter vacation, the laboratory's requirement for every freshman and sophomore is to write an app independently, and I also implemented an app of my own during the winter vacation.

## 2019

+ In 2019, through my university courses and under the guidance of Mr. Jing Chao, I learned Java enterprise application development and got to know back-end tools such as Spring, Spring MVC, MyBatis and Spring Boot. This also let me round out my Android client projects as a whole: Android is the front end, and what you write with Java EE is the back-end server.

+ The same year, after taking a network programming course, I learned about socket communication. This made it much easier for me to make software and hardware talk to each other, and eventually led me into hardware-related knowledge and development. Because our lab needed to enter a competition, I also got into computer vision, and learned how to use OpenCV and its algorithms for binocular stereo vision.

## 2020

+ I am still learning. While picking up new things, I keep going back over the fundamentals I learned before. For a programmer, what matters most is data structures, algorithms and how you use a programming language: starting from the basics of a language, then learning what makes it unique, and finally understanding the data structures and algorithms underneath. Little by little, you discover what a fun thing programming really is.
